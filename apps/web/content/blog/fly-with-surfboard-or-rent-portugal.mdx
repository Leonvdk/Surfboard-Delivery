You've booked the week in the Algarve. Now you're staring at the airline's sports equipment page trying to work out whether your board is coming with you.

Here's the honest arithmetic — the fees, the costs nobody puts in the fee table, and the cases where you absolutely should bring your own board anyway.

## What airlines charge for a surfboard

Fees are per flight, not per trip. That's the first thing people get wrong: whatever number you're looking at, double it.

**Ryanair** — the carrier most people fly into Faro — classes a surfboard as **large sports equipment**: roughly **€55–€65 each way**, with a 20 kg limit and excess charged at about €13/kg per flight. Call it **€110–€130 return** before anything goes wrong. (Published rates as of August 2026 — always confirm at booking, because these move.)

**easyJet** charges a smaller sports-equipment fee, typically around £37 each way when added online, and counts the board against your weight allowance rather than on top of it.

**Legacy carriers — TAP, KLM, Lufthansa, British Airways** — are more varied and often more generous. Some let a board travel *instead of* your checked bag rather than in addition to it, and TAP's charge depends on the route and whether the board is over or under 200 cm. On the right ticket this can be dramatically cheaper than the budget airlines. On the wrong one it isn't.

Two rules that survive every policy change: **book the board in advance online** (airport rates are punitive), and **check the length limit**, because a 9'0 longboard is a different conversation from a 6'6.

## The costs that aren't in the fee table

The fee is the part you can plan for. These are the parts that catch people out:

**The car has to fit the bag.** A standard hire-car booking does not. A 7'0 board bag needs an estate, a van, or roof bars you've booked and paid for separately — and you'll be strapping a board to a rental roof in an airport car park after a 6am flight.

**The oversize desk costs you time at both ends.** Boards don't come out on the belt. Add 20–40 minutes at the oversize claim in Faro, and get to check-in early on the way home.

**Damage happens, and it isn't covered the way you think.** Most carriers limit or exclude liability for sports equipment, and a heel-dent through the deck from a baggage carousel is a repair, not a claim. Anyone who's travelled with boards has a cracked-nose story.

**Weight creeps.** Board bag, board, leash, wax, and a wetsuit hit 20 kg faster than you'd expect. Excess is charged per kilo, per flight.

## When you should absolutely bring your own board

We rent boards for a living and we'll still tell you plainly: sometimes flying with yours is the right call.

- **You're an experienced surfer on a specific shape.** If you ride a particular board and you'd feel the difference on anything else, bring it. A rental you're fighting is worse than an oversize fee.
- **You're staying three weeks or longer.** At some point the rental adds up past the flight cost. Around the two-to-three week mark the maths tips.
- **You're on a legacy carrier where the board replaces your checked bag.** If TAP or KLM will take it inside an allowance you're already paying for, the marginal cost can be small.
- **You're driving, not flying.** From northern Europe by car or van, none of this applies — bring everything.

If you're a beginner or improver, though, or you surf a fairly standard shape, or it's a one-week trip: the board is genuinely not worth the journey. You'll surf the same waves on a 7'8 funboard that's been waxed and checked that morning.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does Ryanair charge for a surfboard to Faro?

As of August 2026, Ryanair prices surfboards as large sports equipment at roughly €55–€65 per flight with a 20 kg limit, so about €110–€130 for a return trip. Book it online in advance; airport prices are higher. Confirm current rates when you book — airline fees change often.

### Can I take a surfboard as my checked bag instead of a suitcase?

On some legacy carriers, yes — TAP, KLM, and others may allow a board to travel within your checked allowance rather than as an extra item, subject to size limits. Budget airlines almost always charge it as a separate sports-equipment item on top of your bag.

### Will a surfboard fit in a rental car in Portugal?

Not in a standard compact. You'll need an estate, an SUV with folding seats, a van, or pre-booked roof bars. Factor that into the hire-car price when you compare flying with a board against renting one here.

### Is it cheaper to rent a surfboard in Portugal than fly with mine?

For a one-week trip, almost always yes. A week's board rental with us is €100 (€150 with a wetsuit) with free delivery, against €110–€130 in airline fees plus a larger hire car. For trips of three weeks or more, or if you need one specific board, bringing your own starts to make sense.

### Do I need to bring a wetsuit to Portugal?

The water on the Costa Vicentina sits around 15–19°C depending on the season, so yes — you'll want a 3/2 or 4/3 most of the year. We include wetsuits in the full package, which saves both the packing weight and a second rental to organise.
