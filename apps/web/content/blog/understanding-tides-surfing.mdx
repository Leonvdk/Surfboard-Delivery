Tides do not create swell, but they reshape the same swell on sand-bottom beaches—an hour can flip a friendly peak into a close-out or the reverse. If the app looked good but the beach did not, tides are often the missing piece.

## What causes tides

The moon’s (and sun’s) gravity pulls on the ocean, creating a rhythmic rise and fall. Most places get roughly **two high tides and two low tides** each lunar day — not exactly 12 hours apart, which is why tide times shift daily.

**Tidal range** matters here: how much vertical difference there is between low and high. Around the Portuguese west coast, range can feel significant on sandbars — enough to change depth over the bank where waves break.

## Spring tides vs neap tides

**Spring tides** (nothing to do with the season) happen around **new moon and full moon**, when sun and moon align. The range is **larger** — higher highs, lower lows. Currents often run stronger; shallow spots get shallower at low.

**Neap tides** happen around **quarter moons**, when lunar and solar pulls partially cancel. The range is **smaller** — less extreme movement, sometimes mellower currents.

Neither is automatically “better” for surfing; they change **how** your local bank works.

## How tides affect wave quality

- **Depth over the sandbar:** More or less water changes how early a wave breaks, how steep it is, and whether it sections.
- **Currents:** River mouths and narrow channels (think **Amoreira**) can push more water on certain stages of tide — rips may strengthen.
- **Hazards:** Low tide can expose rocks or shallow sections that were hidden at high.

The same swell height and period can therefore produce **different shapes** at high vs low — that’s normal.

## Reading a tide chart

1. Pick your **location** nearest the beach (charts are modelled for reference ports; small offsets exist).
2. Read **height** relative to chart datum — what matters for surfing is change over your session as much as absolute number.
3. Note **whether tide is incoming (filling) or outgoing (dropping)** — some banks “turn on” on the push, others on the drop.
4. Check **sunrise/sunset** if light and wind matter for your plan (they usually do in summer).

- Save **one** tide app you like; bouncing between five rarely helps.

## Frequently asked questions

### What tide is best for surfing near Aljezur?

Mid tide is the safest general answer on the beaches around Aljezur, because it usually gives the most workable shape. Arrifana tends to be friendlier for learners from mid to high, Monte Clérigo works across a wide range of the tide, and Amoreira changes character with the river mouth and suits experienced surfers only. These are rules of thumb — the sand shifts through the season, so watch a few sets before you paddle out.

### Is high tide or low tide better for beginners?

Mid to high is often the gentler option on sand beaches. There is more water over the bank, so waves break less abruptly and give you a longer, slower wall of whitewater. It still depends on how that particular bank is sitting that week, so spend five minutes watching from the beach before deciding.

### Why does the tide time change every day?

Because the lunar day is about 24 hours and 50 minutes rather than 24. High and low tides shift forward by roughly 50 minutes each day as a result. That is why yesterday's dawn low tide is a mid-morning low today, and why you cannot reuse last week's timings.

### Do spring tides mean bigger waves?

No. Swell size comes from storms out in the Atlantic, not from the moon. Spring tides — around new and full moon — mean a larger range between high and low, so more water moves in the same six hours. That changes wave shape and can make currents stronger, but it does not add swell.

### How do I tell if the tide is coming in or going out?

Read the hourly curve on a tide chart rather than just the high and low times. What matters for a session is which way the water is moving and how much it will change while you are out. Some banks turn on as the tide fills, others as it drops, so note the direction and see how the waves respond over your first half hour.

### Should I avoid surfing at low tide?

Not as a rule — plenty of spots are at their best on a low. Avoid it when low tide exposes shallow rock, or turns the wave into a dumping shorebreak that is beyond your level. If the beach looks wrong when you arrive, move to another one or wait for the push.
